## Who this is for

Every entry is judged for **solo and side-project work** — one person
building something, paying for it themselves. A tool that is excellent
for a funded team can still land in `hold` here. If you are evaluating
for an organization with a procurement process, the rings will read
backwards to you; the catalog itself may still be useful.

## What the rings mean

- **Adopt** — proven in real use, a default choice.
- **Trial** — actively being tried on a real project.
- **Assess** — worth knowing about, not yet tried.
- **Hold** — not a fit for solo work, with the reason recorded.

The distinction that matters: `adopt` and `trial` describe *experience*,
`assess` and `hold` describe *research*. Nothing reaches `adopt` or
`trial` by being researched — that requires actually having used it, and
is always a human decision. So a newly discovered tool starts at
`assess`, however good it looks.

## How assess and hold are decided

A tool is **assess** when all of these hold: a free tier or hobby pricing
under roughly $20/month; self-serve signup with no sales call; docs
written for individual developers rather than procurement; and value you
can see at small scale.

It is **hold** when any of these hold: enterprise-only or "contact us"
pricing; no free tier and entry pricing above roughly $20/month; needs
significant existing infrastructure before you can even evaluate it;
solves a problem that only exists at organization scale; or a free tier
too constrained to carry a real side project.

`hold` is not a verdict on quality. It records that the economics or the
shape of the product do not fit one person building alone.

## How the dial is arranged

Sectors are **problem areas** — what you are trying to solve, not what
category the vendor sells into. Distance from the centre is the ring.

A tool appears in **every area it serves**, so one tool can be several
dots. That is why the dot count runs ahead of the tool count. Within a
sector, dots fill outward from the inner edge of their band; position
inside a band carries no meaning beyond packing.

## What is deliberately missing

Out of scope, and never added: courses and education, consultancies and
dev shops, hiring marketplaces, general productivity apps, and lifestyle
products. The test is whether you integrate it into a project or tool
your development workflow with it directly.

Absence is not a judgment either — it usually just means it has not been
researched yet.
